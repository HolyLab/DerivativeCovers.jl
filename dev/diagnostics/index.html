DerivativeCovers.Diagnostics — Module
DerivativeCovers.Diagnostics

Tools for measuring the cost of a loose cover.

Nothing here requires a Tape or any other package-specific type. These functions act on bare covers h, gradients g, and matrices A, so they accept covers from any source.

The two halves compose. An InflationPattern distorts a reference cover by a controlled amount and shape — inflate only ever weakens a bound, so the distortion is still a valid cover of the same matrix. The consumers (cover_norm, regularized_step, psd_shift, cover_cg) then use a cover as a metric on the variables, and each has a paired form (stopping_shift, regularization_bias, psd_excess, step_degradation) that report what the distortion costs. Because every consumer is scale-invariant, the degradation is attributable to looseness alone.

InflationPattern

How an inflation is distributed across coordinates. Subtypes supply a shape via inflation_shape, which inflation_factors raises to a peak factor: UniformInflation, LognormalInflation, SpikeInflation, RampInflation.

DerivativeCovers.Diagnostics.LognormalInflation — Type
LognormalInflation()

Log-factors proportional to abs(z) for i.i.d. standard normal z, rescaled so the largest is the requested peak. The factors are therefore lognormal in shape but exact in magnitude: the draw decides which coordinates are loose and how sharply, never how loose the worst one is.

For large n the maximum of the draw runs well ahead of the bulk, so the profile is a concentrated head over coordinates that stay near 1 — the anisotropy regime seen on the deep and DAG families. Contrast UniformInflation, the plateau regime.

source
DerivativeCovers.Diagnostics.RampInflation — Type
RampInflation()

Log-factors graded linearly with position, from 1 at the first coordinate to the peak at the last. Coordinate order is the ramp's axis, so permute the cover (or the problem) to choose which coordinates take the looseness.

DerivativeCovers.Diagnostics.SpikeInflation — Type
SpikeInflation(index)

One coordinate inflated to the peak, every other left exactly at 1. The extreme point of anisotropy at a given peak, and the pattern that isolates a single coordinate's contribution to a consumer's degradation. index must be a valid index into the cover.

DerivativeCovers.Diagnostics.UniformInflation — Type
UniformInflation()

Every coordinate inflated by the same factor. The isotropic reference: anisotropy of its factors is exactly zero, so it is the control against which the anisotropic patterns are read.

DerivativeCovers.Diagnostics.anisotropy — Method
anisotropy(f::AbstractVector) -> a

Spread of the inflation factors f, as the log ratio of their largest to their geometric mean. It is zero exactly when every coordinate is inflated alike, and grows as the same peak inflation is concentrated on fewer coordinates: at peak p over n coordinates, UniformInflation gives 0, RampInflation about log(p)/2, and SpikeInflation log(p)*(1 - 1/n).

Unlike the peak, this is a property of the realized profile rather than of the request, so it is the strength axis to plot a consumer's degradation against when the pattern is random.

DerivativeCovers.Diagnostics.cover_cg — Method
cover_cg(A, b, h; x0=zero(b), maxiter=length(b), reltol=sqrt(eps()), reset=16)
    -> (; x, iters, xs)

Conjugate gradients on A*x = b, preconditioned by the cover: the residual is measured in the metric Diagonal(h.^2) that the cover certifies, making the search directions invariant under rescaling of the variables. Returns the final iterate x, the number of iterations iters, and the history xs of every iterate from x0 on, which is what lets a caller judge two different covers by one common yardstick.

A cover that matches A's curvature scales makes the scaled system well conditioned, so the iteration count is a direct read on cover quality: an exact cover of a diagonal A converges in one step, and rescaling that cover by a constant changes nothing, since only the shape of the metric steers the iteration. Iteration stops when the cover-metric residual norm falls to reltol times its initial value, or after maxiter iterations — which is the only backstop, as that norm is not monotone in the iteration and a badly scaled metric makes it visibly non-monotone. Every reset iterations the residual is recomputed from x rather than updated, to keep rounding error from accumulating.

Note that the convergence test is itself stated in the cover's metric, so two runs under different covers stop at genuinely different accuracies; comparing them requires a metric fixed outside the iteration, as step_degradation does.

Requires a strictly positive cover, and a symmetric positive definite A. Definiteness is enforced as the iteration goes: a search direction of nonpositive curvature throws rather than producing the NaN iterates that dividing by it would.

DerivativeCovers.Diagnostics.cover_norm — Method
cover_norm(g, h) -> r

Norm of the gradient g measured in the metric of the cover h, norm(g ./ h). This is the scale-invariant convergence test: it is dimensionless (each g[i]/h[i] divides a gradient by a curvature scale) and unchanged when the variables are rescaled.

A cover coordinate may be exactly zero, which says the Hessian ignores that input. The norm is then Inf if g[i] is nonzero — no step along i is justified by curvature, so the test cannot be satisfied — and the coordinate is simply skipped if g[i] vanishes too.

DerivativeCovers.Diagnostics.inflate — Method
inflate(h::AbstractVector, f::AbstractVector) -> hd
inflate(pattern, h::AbstractVector, peak; rng=Xoshiro(0)) -> hd

Loosen the cover h coordinatewise by the factors f (or by the factors that pattern and peak generate, per inflation_factors). Since every factor is at least 1, hd covers every matrix h covers.

A coordinate with h[i] == 0 — which a valid cover has wherever the Hessian ignores that input — stays at zero: a multiplicative distortion cannot loosen a bound that is already exact. Factors below 1 are rejected, as they could break the cover.

DerivativeCovers.Diagnostics.inflation_factors — Method
inflation_factors(pattern, h::AbstractVector, peak; rng=Xoshiro(0)) -> f
inflation_factors(pattern, n::Integer, peak; rng=Xoshiro(0)) -> f

Per-coordinate inflation factors f = peak .^ inflation_shape(pattern, h), laid out on the axes of h (or as a length-n vector). Every factor lies in [1, peak], and the largest equals peak exactly, whatever the pattern; peak == 1 gives the identity distortion.

f is the realized per-coordinate profile of the distortion, so a caller that wants to report what it applied should keep f and summarize it (with anisotropy, say) rather than recompute it from the inflated cover — a cover coordinate that is exactly zero stays zero under inflate and reveals nothing about the factor it was multiplied by.

DerivativeCovers.Diagnostics.inflation_shape — Function
inflation_shape(pattern, h::AbstractVector; rng=Xoshiro(0)) -> w

The pattern's shape over the coordinates of h: 0 <= w <= 1 with maximum(w) == 1, sharing the axes of h. rng is used only by the random patterns.

DerivativeCovers.Diagnostics.psd_excess — Method
psd_excess(A, h, hd) -> (; tau_ref, tau_dist, excess)

How much extra shift an inflated cover forces the PSD boost to apply. tau_ref and tau_dist are the minimal shifts of psd_shift under the reference cover h and its distortion hd, and excess[i] is the ratio of the shift actually applied to coordinate i to the shift the reference boost applies there,

excess[i] = (tau_dist * hd[i]^2) / (tau_ref * h[i]^2)

which is at least 1, and equals 1 for every coordinate exactly when the distortion is uniform: scaling a cover by a constant leaves the shift matrix tau*Diagonal(h.^2) unchanged, so this consumer is unaffected by isotropic looseness and responds only to anisotropy. Summarize the profile with maximum for the worst-hit coordinate. When A is already positive semidefinite both shifts are zero and the excess is 1 throughout.

source
DerivativeCovers.Diagnostics.psd_shift — Method
psd_shift(A, h) -> tau

Smallest tau >= 0 for which A + tau*Diagonal(h.^2) is positive semidefinite: the cheapest PSD boost expressed in the units the cover certifies. It is the negative part of the smallest eigenvalue of A scaled into cover coordinates, A[i,j]/(h[i]*h[j]), and so is invariant under rescaling of the variables.

Requires a strictly positive cover, since the scaling divides by it. A must be symmetric.

DerivativeCovers.Diagnostics.regularization_bias — Method
regularization_bias(A, g, h, hd, tau) -> b

How far an inflated cover moves the regularized step. h is the reference cover of A and hd a distortion of it; the bias is the distance between the two steps of regularized_step, relative to the reference step, both measured in the reference cover's metric:

b = norm(h .* (x_dist - x_ref)) / norm(h .* x_ref)

Comparing the two regularized steps — rather than either against the unregularized A \ (-g) — isolates the effect of cover looseness from the effect of regularizing at all, since A and tau are held fixed and only the cover changes.

source
DerivativeCovers.Diagnostics.regularized_step — Method
regularized_step(A, g, h, tau) -> x

Step of the regularized system (A + tau*Diagonal(h.^2)) * x = -g. The shift tau*Diagonal(h.^2) carries the units of the curvature the cover certifies, so tau is dimensionless and a single value of it means the same thing on every problem.

DerivativeCovers.Diagnostics.step_degradation — Method
step_degradation(A, g, h, hd; maxiter=length(g), target=1e-6, kwargs...)
    -> (; iters_ref, iters_dist, error_ref, error_dist)

What an inflated cover costs the preconditioned Newton step of cover_cg on A*x = -g. Both runs are judged in the reference cover's metric, against the exact step A \ (-g):

error(x) = norm(h .* (x - A \ (-g))) / norm(h .* (A \ (-g)))

iters_ref and iters_dist are the iterations each run needs to bring that error below target, or nothing if maxiter iterations do not suffice — which is itself the degradation, a step budget the distorted cover cannot deliver on. error_ref and error_dist are the errors of the final iterates, and are the reading to take when the budget, not the accuracy, is what is fixed.

Holding the yardstick fixed matters: cover_cg's own convergence test lives in whichever metric it was handed, so a loose cover would otherwise flatter itself by declaring victory sooner. Remaining keyword arguments are forwarded to cover_cg.

DerivativeCovers.Diagnostics.stopping_index — Method
stopping_index(grads, covers, tol) -> k

Index of the first iterate along a trajectory at which the convergence test cover_norm(grads[k], covers[k]) <= tol fires, or nothing if it never does.

grads and covers are the gradients and covers at successive iterates of an optimization run (a single cover may be repeated when it is held fixed along the path). tol is likewise data describing that run—the convergence threshold tested—not a parameter controlling the accuracy of the returned index.

DerivativeCovers.Diagnostics.stopping_shift — Method
stopping_shift(grads, covers, dcovers, tol) -> (; k_ref, k_dist, shift, overshoot)

What an inflated cover costs the convergence test. covers are the reference covers along the trajectory grads and dcovers their distortions; k_ref and k_dist are the iterates at which cover_norm first falls to tol under each.

An inflated cover shrinks the measured norm, so the distorted test always fires no later than the reference one: shift = k_ref - k_dist >= 0 counts the iterations it stops early, and overshoot >= 1 is the true cover-norm at that premature stop divided by tol — how far the run really was from the requested tolerance when it declared success.

tol is data describing the analyzed run, alongside grads and covers — the convergence threshold tested in that run, not a parameter controlling the accuracy of the returned shift.

Throws if the trajectory is too short for either test to fire; extend it or loosen tol.
